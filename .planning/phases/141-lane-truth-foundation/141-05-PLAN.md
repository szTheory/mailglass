<objective>
Make "no `ci.yml` job sits unclassified" a build-breaking fact rather than a claim, and machine-enforce
the two hazards a comment alone cannot hold: the runtime matrix-name suffix and byte-exact string
comparison across four language surfaces.

Purpose: ROADMAP criterion 2 says no job can silently sit in neither `ci_green.needs` nor
`gate-ci-green`'s recognized set. Plan 141-04 chose — deliberately and on the record — to have
`gate-ci-green` only *warn* on an unclassified-but-green lane, so that release-time posture is preserved
byte-for-byte. The **hard failure therefore has to live here**, in the meta-test that runs on the PR
which adds the lane. This plan is where criterion 2 stops being a policy and becomes an assertion.

Output: five hardening assertions plus a negative control on
`test/scripts/lane_classification_drift_test.exs`.

**Decisions implemented here:** D-06 (the meta-test carries an anti-vacuity guard in the style of
`required_checks_test.exs:30-34` and `:102-107` — a parser that silently matches nothing must fail
loudly, not pass; every assertion added here is hand-rolled with **no new dependency**) · D-02 (the
three-bucket gating model plus the structural bucket is what "every job is classified" is asserted
against) · D-01 (`Mailglass.CILanes` is read as the single source; no lane list is duplicated in the
test).

## Decisions recorded here (never made by omission)

**The gate warns; the meta-test fails.** `gate-ci-green` (plan 141-04) emits `core.warning` on an
unclassified-but-green lane and `core.setFailed` on an unclassified-but-red one — exactly today's
release behavior plus a diagnostic. This plan's set-equality assertion is the **hard** gate: an
unclassified lane cannot be merged in the first place, because the meta-test that runs in `ci.yml`'s
`mix_task_tests` job fails on it. The split is deliberate: a hard failure at release time on a *green*
lane is a foot-gun; a hard failure on the PR that introduces the lane is the correct place and time.
