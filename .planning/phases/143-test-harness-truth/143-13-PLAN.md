<objective>
Give the two Core Full Suite gating legs veto power over a Hex publish, with a self-heal that keeps the
hands-free release pipeline from deadlocking and an override that keeps a blocked release affordable.

The trade, argued once and recorded. Against gating: the ecosystem norm is dramatically weaker — comparable
Elixir libraries publish on tag push with no test gating at all — and on a hands-free auto-merging pipeline a
wedged gate is a silent unattended stall. For gating: the seven required lanes are narrow contract and file
lists, so today a total core regression can reach Hex without a single red light. The asymmetry decides it. A
blocked release costs the maintainer thirty minutes and one dispatch; a published broken core costs every
adopter and cannot be unpublished after sixty minutes on Hex. Gate — and pay for it with a documented
override rather than by narrowing the gate.

Only the two Elixir 1.18 / OTP 27 legs gate, matching the declared version floor. The next-toolchain legs,
provider compatibility, and the inbound suite stay advisory: classified, enumerated, warned on, never
blocking. This repo's own prior research settles the scope — keep required matrices small; one gigantic
matrix as a required status is a recorded anti-pattern.

Purpose: HARNESS-04, sequenced strictly after the promotion checkpoint.
Output: a dual-workflow self-heal, two classification arrays, a decision table, a dispatch-only override, and
drift assertions binding all of it to the registry.
</objective>
